The insurance requirements section of a commercial subcontract for SPF work has gotten progressively more demanding over the past decade. $1M/$2M GL used to be sufficient for most projects. Today, large commercial GCs and institutional owners routinely require SPF subcontractors to carry umbrella limits of $5M or $10M — and sometimes more.

Here's how commercial umbrella coverage works for SPF contractors, and what to watch for when your GC hands you an insurance requirement sheet.

## What a Commercial Umbrella Does

A commercial umbrella policy provides excess liability coverage above the limits of your underlying policies — typically GL, employer's liability (within WC), and commercial auto.

When an underlying policy is exhausted — a claim exceeds the $1M GL per-occurrence limit, for example — the umbrella takes over and pays up to its own limit. A $5M umbrella above a $1M GL policy gives you total coverage of $6M per occurrence.

The umbrella also typically provides drop-down coverage: if the underlying policy fails to respond (due to an exclusion or coverage dispute), the umbrella may step in and pay — subject to a self-insured retention.

## Follow-Form Coverage

The most important concept for SPF contractors evaluating umbrella policies is follow-form coverage.

A follow-form umbrella adopts the terms and conditions of the underlying GL policy. If your underlying GL is written with SPF-specific endorsements — off-ratio coverage, isocyanate classification, correct class codes — then a follow-form umbrella extends those terms to the umbrella limit.

A non-follow-form umbrella (sometimes called a "stand-alone" umbrella) has its own terms and conditions. It might exclude work your SPF GL specifically covers — like off-ratio events or certain chemical exposures. When a claim triggers the umbrella, the umbrella's terms control — and if those terms exclude SPF work, the umbrella won't respond.

We write umbrella policies for SPF contractors that follow form over the SPF-specific GL. When reviewing umbrella quotes from other markets, we verify that the umbrella form doesn't reintroduce exclusions that the underlying GL endorsements specifically address.

## $5M vs. $10M Umbrella Requirements

GC insurance requirements for SPF subcontractors vary by project type and owner requirements:

**$1M GL only:** Small residential projects, owner-builders, limited commercial.

**$2M GL + $3M umbrella ($5M total):** Common for mid-market commercial SPF projects — office buildings, warehouses, multifamily.

**$1M GL + $4M umbrella or $2M GL + $8M umbrella ($5M–$10M total):** Large commercial, industrial, institutional (hospitals, schools, government buildings).

**$10M+ total:** Federal projects, large industrial facilities, high-value commercial — sometimes require $10M umbrella above $1M or $2M GL.

The certificate of insurance must show the umbrella limit and list the GC as additional insured on both the GL and umbrella.

## The CPL Stack

One frequently overlooked issue for SPF contractors: whether the umbrella also stacks over CPL.

If your GC requires $5M total for pollution liability — not just GL — then the umbrella must follow form over CPL as well. Some umbrella markets will write excess over both GL and CPL; others will only excess over GL.

For large commercial SPF projects where isocyanate exposure risk is elevated — occupied buildings, projects near HVAC systems, dense commercial environments — stacking umbrella over CPL provides meaningful additional protection. We identify markets that will write the full stack.

## Certificate Compliance for GC Requirements

GC insurance requirement sheets often contain specific language about how the certificate must read. Common requirements:

- GC listed as additional insured on GL and umbrella
- Waiver of subrogation in favor of GC
- Primary and non-contributory language on GL
- Umbrella must be "follow form" to underlying GL
- Certificate holder language may be project-specific

We review GC certificate requirements before binding and confirm the policy forms satisfy each requirement. Certificate language that doesn't match the actual policy creates problems at claims time — we build the policy first to satisfy requirements, then issue the certificate.

## Umbrella Costs for SPF Contractors

Umbrella premium for SPF contractors is driven by:
- Total underlying premium (umbrella carriers follow the underlying premium as a rating factor)
- Umbrella limit required ($5M vs. $10M)
- Application type and completed-operations exposure
- Loss history on underlying policies

For most SPF contractors, $5M umbrella premium ranges from $2,000 to $5,000 annually above the GL and CPL premium. $10M umbrella adds roughly $3,000 to $7,000. The exact premium depends on the full account profile.

For contractors actively bidding commercial work that requires $5M or $10M umbrella, the umbrella is rarely the budget obstacle. The coverage is cost-effective relative to the contract value it enables you to pursue.
